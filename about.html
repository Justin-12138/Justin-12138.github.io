About — the longer version

A slow path
into the stack.

Robotics graduate, AI engineer, skateboarder, English learner. Still early in all of it.

Guangzhou, China · 23°N
B.E. Robotics Engineering, GUET

I'm Justin Liu, an AI engineer in Guangzhou. I studied robotics engineering at Guilin University of Electronic Technology, did a short stint as a data analyst, and now work on LLM systems at Isoftstone.

My path into this wasn't a straight line. Robotics led to machine learning, machine learning led to data, and data led to the infrastructure underneath models: how they are served, how they retrieve, how they fail when the document is longer than the prompt budget.

I learn best by reading source code, running the benchmark, and writing down where the clean explanation stops matching the actual system. This site is where those notes accumulate.

I'm not a researcher with a long publication list, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. I'm an engineer who likes the part where the demo stops working.

01 / The path

Not a straight
line.

A few turns, one thread: I like systems that have to work, not just demo.

  1. 01

    Robotics first

    GUET · 2021–2025

    Control loops, sensors, computer vision and traditional machine learning. Robotics gave me the maths and the habit of treating a model as one part of a larger system.

  2. 02

    Data, and reproducing papers

    Tecdata · 2022–2023

    A short remote stint as a data analyst: text-rule mining, Poisson regression, and reproducing published methods across finance, environment and disease data. A useful lesson in how much depends on cleaning and assumptions.

  3. 03

    Causal inference

    GUET · 2023–2024

    GEO data, MRMR, CFS and causal feature selection for Alzheimer's biomarkers. The work became a first-author paper at ICAI 2024. What stayed with me: a result is easy; defensible evidence is slow.

  4. 04

    Inference and serving

    Isoftstone · 2024–present

    Benchmarking vLLM, SGLang and LMDeploy; KV-cache reuse with LMCache and Dynamo; DP/TP/PP/EP trade-offs; a LlamaFactory-based training platform. Learning by measurement, and discovering how many benchmark results depend on the benchmark.

  5. 05

    Retrieval and documents

    Isoftstone · 2024–2025

    Turning long reports into document trees, parsing with VLMs, and evaluating retrieval by whether it produces evidence — not by whether the top-k results look relevant. The long version lives in the notes.

  6. 06

    Now: multimodal and agents

    Exploring · ongoing

    VLM and OCR pipelines, agent frameworks, and the systems work that makes them usable. I'm more interested in the failure modes than in the launch post.
